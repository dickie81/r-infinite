# Ideas register: an exponent below 7/3 for prime moduli

README round 546 opens this register. The owner's instruction (2026-10-18) was: "Keep searching, do not give up until every idea is exhausted".

The goal is a density exponent below `7/3` near `σ = 5/7` for the characters mod a prime `q`, with `T = q^{o(1)}`. Through the stack's half-plane and round 520's `Linnik.linnik_of_charDensity`, that is a Linnik exponent below `7/3`.

Each idea carries one status:

- **CORE**: open, and tied to a named open problem; listed under "The cores".
- **OPEN**: no closing argument yet; more work is owed.
- **REDUCED**: shown to come down to a core.
- **CLOSED**: a recorded argument shows the idea cannot reach the goal alone, in the scope stated.
- **SURVEYED**: the literature was checked and nothing usable was found.

A closure is a statement about the idea as described, in the scope stated. It is not a claim that no variant can work. `idea_register.py` checks every quantitative closure marked [R*] in exact arithmetic.

## A. Zero detection

- **A1. Littlewood's lemma with a mollified second moment (Selberg's method).** CLOSED [R1, R15c]. In the form `N(σ) ≪ q^{1−(2σ−1)θ}`, with a mollifier of length `q^θ`, the exponent at `σ = 5/7` is below `7/3` only for `θ > 7/9`. At `θ = 1/2` it is `11/4`. The explicit formula with test functions of Fourier support `s` would give the same exponent at `θ = s/2`, and `2` at `s = 2` for every `σ`, if every zero off the line contributed positively. They need not: for the Fejér kernel `φ(x) = (sin πsx/πx)²`, the weight `2 Re φ(x + iy)` of an off-line zero changes sign as `x`, its scaled ordinate, varies, once `|y|` is large. Even at `θ = 51/101`, the twist length up to which Bui–Pratt–Robles–Zaharescu obtain the twisted second moment at the central point (C14), the formula gives `277/101 ≈ 2.743` [R17a].
- **A2. Turán's power sums and the Halász–Turán method.** CLOSED at `σ = 5/7` for the bounds recorded with a range [R11]. The exponent database (§11.2) records Turán's `A(1 − η) ≤ 2 + η^{0.14}` for `η` small enough, and Halász–Turán bounds `A(1 − η) ≤ Cη^{1/2}` for sufficiently small `η`. Its Table 11.2 gives three with a range. Ford's `58.05(1 − σ)^{1/2}`, stated for `1/2 ≤ σ ≤ 1`, gives more than `31` at `σ = 5/7`. Heath-Brown's `6.42(1 − σ)^{1/2}` and Pintz's `3√2(1 − σ)^{1/2} + 18(1 − σ)` are stated for `σ ≥ 9/10` and `σ ≥ 17/18`. These are bounds for `ζ`. This round did not reconstruct the method for the characters mod `q` at `σ = 5/7`.
- **A3. Detectors from `L′/L` with Heath-Brown's identity.** CLOSED as a route around the critical length. The smooth factors of the identity have arbitrary lengths, so smooth sums of length near `q^{7/18}` reappear. Detection through the explicit formula needs Turán's lemma (A2).
- **A4. A heavier mollifier, splitting each block into `d·m` pieces.** CLOSED. The piece `d ≍ 1` is the smooth sum of length `N` itself, which cannot be factored. A large value of the block can sit on that piece.
- **A5. Products of the critical block with other blocks.** CLOSED. A product is counted only when each factor is large. A zero detected by the critical block alone gives only the powers of that block, of lengths `7/9` and `7/6`, at the two ends of the window.

- **B16. Flat restricted isometry with an energy condition (round 554).** CLOSED in its direct form [R19b]. Its proof applies the flat bound only to subsets of the support, so it holds inside `W`.
  - With the unit columns of B10, a flat sum is `(Σw)^{−1}Σ_{a∈A,b∈B}K(a − b)`, where `K` is the Gram kernel, and `Σ_m K(m) = 0`.   - Large-values sets can hold longer ones. Take `b_n = φ(θ_n)e(−lθ_n²/2)` on `I = [N, 2N]`, with `θ_n = ind(n)/L` and `φ` a smooth bump equal to `1` on `[3/16, 13/16]`. Expanding in Fourier series, `Σ_n b_nχ_j(n) = |I|c_j(0) + Σ_{h≢0} c_j(h)Σ_{n∈I}χ_h(n)`. By stationary phase `|c_j(0)| = l^{−1/2} + O(1/l)` for `l/4 ≤ j ≤ 3l/4`, and `Σ_h |c_j(h)| = O(√l)`. With Pólya–Vinogradov the window is large at `N^{5/7}` for `l ≍ N/(√q log q)`, a progression of length about `q^{5/18}/log q`.

Add a small multiple `ε ≍ q^{−1/12}` of a chirp, with `l` a small constant times `N/(√q log q)` and its window placed where the set's transform is below `2V`. At most `1.24|I|` was measured. What closes is the direct route, not a variant that treats structured parts of `W` separately.

- **B19. The Gauss-sum transfer: an `N`-free form of B10 (round 555).** CLOSED as a route, recorded for its identity [R20b]. Writing the weight as `w(y) = Σ_ξ c_ξe(ξy/q)` gives `K(ψ) = τ(ψ)Σ_{ξ≠0}c_ξψ̄(ξ)` for `ψ ≠ χ₀`. So the off-diagonal Gram matrix is `E_W = Σ_{ξ≠0}c_ξD_ξ^*T′_WD_ξ`, with `T′_W` the matrix of Gauss sums `τ(χχ̄′)` off the diagonal and `D_ξ = diag(χ(ξ))`, and `‖E_W‖ ≤ C‖T′_W‖` with `C = Σ_{ξ≠0}|c_ξ|`.

- **B20. The Hadamard square of the block's Gram matrix (round 556).** CLOSED as a route [R21a]. Let `w` be the smooth weight of the block (length `N₀`) and `G(χ, χ′) = Σ_n w(n)χχ̄′(n)` its Gram matrix on `W`. The multiplicative convolution `w ∗ w` on `[N₀², 4N₀²]` has Gram matrix `G ∘ G`, the entrywise square, since `Σ_m (w ∗ w)(m)ψ(m) = (Σ_n w(n)ψ(n))²`.
  - For `|b_n| ≤ w(n)`, duality with this weight gives `Σ_{χ∈W}|S_b(χ)|⁴ ≤ λ_max(G ∘ G)(Σ_n w(n))²`. Schur's product theorem gives `λ_max(G ∘ G) ≤ (Σ_n w(n))λ_max(G)`.
  - At the critical point a large-values set needs `λ_max(G ∘ G) ≥ |W|V₀⁴/N₀² = q`, so Schur's bound needs `λ_max(G) ≥ q^{11/18}`.
  - Why it closes nothing: the block's own large values already need `λ_max(G) ≥ |W|V₀²/N₀ = q^{5/6}`, and the large sieve allows up to `q + N₀`. The Hadamard structure adds no constraint.
- **B21. The large-spectrum energy bound (round 556).** CLOSED: vacuous at the critical block [R21b]. Suppose `|Σ_{x∈A}χ̄(x)| ≥ δ|A|` for every `χ ∈ W`, with `A ⊂ F_q^*`. Hölder with `4/3` and `4` on `Σ_{x∈A}Σ_{χ∈W}c_χχ̄(x)`, `|c_χ| = 1`, gives `E(W) ≥ δ⁴|A||W|⁴/L`, that is `κ ≥ δ⁴|A|`.

  - B18 is the effective form. Removing the diagonal and using that the block's ratios do not wrap gives `κ − 1 ≥ q^{−1/9}`.
- **B22. Chen–Gupta–Li's Lemma 10.5 at the block (round 556).** CLOSED [R21c]. Their §10 introduces it with "The following lemma establishes that the energy of W can be bounded in terms of the discrete third moment of R." (PDF text layer, spaces restored). Its display bounds `E(W)` by `N^{−2σ}` times the sum over `n₁, n₂ ∼ N` of `|R|³` at the ratio `n₁/n₂`.

- **B23. The energy squeeze at exact criticality (round 556).** CLOSED [R21d]. In the proof of Proposition 10.2: "E(W) ⪅ N^{−2σ}(N|W|³ + N(qT)^{1/4}|W|^{21/8} + E(W)^{1/2}|W|^{1/2}N²)" (PDF text layer, spaces and exponents restored). The last term gives their first term `|W|N^{4−4σ}`.

  - Write `E(W) = |W|⁴/L + X`. The diagonal is the least energy times the mean value theorem's slack `L‖b‖²/(|W|V²)`. If the off-diagonal pairs spread uniformly over `F_q^*`, the off-diagonal is at most `N²|W|^{1/2}X^{1/2}`. The spread is a heuristic here; Chen–Gupta–Li treat the distribution with their gcd lemmas.

  - Why it closes nothing: the density argument must rule out `|W| = q^{2/3−d}` for small `d > 0`, and also sets a constant factor below the mean value bound. The squeeze bites only when `|W|` is within a factor `1 + q^{−1/9}` of the mean value bound.
- **B24. The trilinear Jacobi form as a convolution (round 556).** CLOSED: circular. Fix `χ₂` in the Gauss-sum form of SEVEN-THIRDS §6. The sum over `ψ₁, ψ₂` carries `J(ψ₁, ψ₂)F(ψ̄₁)F(ψ̄₂)F(ψ₁ψ₂)`, and `J(ψ₁, ψ₂) = τ(ψ₁)τ(ψ₂)/τ(ψ₁ψ₂)` when `ψ₁`, `ψ₂` and `ψ₁ψ₂` are nontrivial.

  - That transform is the weight again, concentrated where `−x̄` lies in `[N, 2N]`. The second Poisson summation undoes the first, and the form returns to the Gram matrix of the interval.
- **B25. The Gauss-sum matrix by its moments (round 557).** REDUCED to core 1, through B19 [R22a]. `‖T′_W‖^{2k} ≤ tr((T′_WT′_W^*)^k)`, a sum over closed walks of length `2k` in `W` of products of Gauss sums.
  - The walks whose steps pair up as in a tree give `C_kq^k|W|^{k+1}` (`C_k` the Catalan number), the free-probability value for compressing a unitary with uniform spectrum. If the other walks were no larger, then `‖T′_W‖ ≤ Cq^{1/2}|W|^{(k+1)/(2k)}`: no saving at `k = 2`, `q^{17/18}` at `k = 3`, tending to `q^{5/6}`. With B19 a large-values set at the critical point would then have `|W| ≤ q^{11/18}` (from `k = 3` at `|W| = q^{2/3}`), and `|W| ≤ q^{1/2}` after iterating.
  - At `k = 3` the other walks give `q` times a sum, over a four-dimensional linear section `{Σ_i ±s_i = 0}`, of products of six values of `R` at the ratios `s_i/s_{i+1}`. The tree part is `5q³|W|⁴ = 5q^{17/3}`. Replacing `|R|` by its typical size `|W|^{1/2}` gives `q⁷`, a factor `q^{4/3}` above, and square-root cancellation would give `q⁵`.
  - Why it stops: the bound needs about two-thirds of square-root cancellation in that section, for every `W` of minimal energy. At `k = 2` the corresponding two-dimensional sum `Σ_{x,x′}R(x)R̄(x′)R̄((1 − x)/(1 − x′))R(x′(1 − x)/(x(1 − x′)))` is bounded by Cauchy–Schwarz, and by Hölder with the restricted fourth moments, only at `q²|W|² = q^{10/3}`, above its tree part `q|W|³ = q³`. Energy information gives no cancellation in these sums. *(Round 558: two-thirds is what matching the free value needs. To bring the other walks below `q⁶` at all, and so save a power in `‖T′_W‖`, the route needs a little more than half of square-root cancellation in that section, and `k = 3` is the moment that needs the least share [R23a]. - **B26. Sum-product through multiplicative energies (round 557).** CLOSED [R22b]. If `f ∈ span(W)` has a fraction `c` of its mass on the interval `I`, then `g = f·1_I` has `‖P_Wg‖ ≥ c`. So its multiplicative `k`-energy `E_k(g) = (1/L)Σ_χ|ĝ(χ)|^{2k}` is at least `c^{2k}(L/|W|)^{k−1} = c^{2k}q^{(k−1)/3}`. A sum-product argument would try to show that functions on an interval have smaller `k`-energy.
  - Why it closes nothing: the interval's own normalized `k`-energy is about `N^k/q`, because products of `k` elements of `I` wrap around modulo `q`. That is at least `q^{(k−1)/3}` for every `k ≥ 2` (the exponents meet at `k = 3/2`), so spreading `g` over `I` meets every energy condition.
  - Only a support of size `s` below `q^{(k+2)/(3k)}` (`q^{2/3}` at `k = 2`) needs multiplicative structure beyond wrapping, which is where sum-product tools act. A critical configuration can be spread over the whole interval, and then the condition is the large-values problem itself with flat coefficients.

- **B27. The six-cycle as a bilinear form (round 558).** REDUCED to core 1, through B19 and B25 [R23b]. Let `T_W = [τ(χχ̄′)]_{χ,χ′∈W}` (diagonal `τ(χ₀) = −1`, so `T′_W = T_W + I`) and `D = diag(χ(−1))`. Since `conj(τ(ψ)) = ψ(−1)τ(ψ̄)`, `T_W^* = DT_WD`, so `T_WD` is Hermitian and `tr((T_WT_W^*)³) = ‖(T_WD)³‖_F²`.
  - Expanding the three Gauss sums and summing over the two middle characters gives `tr((T_WT_W^*)³) = q·Σ_{χ₁≠χ₄}|S(χ₁, χ₄)|² + Σ_{χ∈W}|Z(χ) − S(χ, χ)|²`. Here `S(χ₁, χ₄) = Σ_{x+y+z=1, xyz≠0} χ₁(x)χ̄₄(z)R(−y/x)R(−z/y)`, and `Z(χ) = L·Σ_{u≠0,−1} χ̄(u)R(1 + u)R(u/(1 + u))` comes from the triples that sum to `0`.
  - The diagonal part is harmless. It is at most `2Σ_{χ∈W}|Z(χ)|² + 2Σ_{χ∈W}|S(χ, χ)|²`. Orthogonality in `u` and Cauchy–Schwarz give `Σ_{χ∈W}|Z(χ)|² ≤ L³Σ_x|R(x)|⁴ = κL³|W|⁴`, which is `q^{17/3+o(1)}` when `κ ≤ q^{o(1)}` (Proposition 10.2), the size of B25's tree part. The second sum is at most the `q^{16/3}` of the next bullet.
  - `S` is the two-dimensional multiplicative Fourier transform of `G(x, z) = R(−y/x)R(−z/y)`, with `y = 1 − x − z`. The map `(x, z) ↦ (−y/x, −z/y)` is injective and `Σ_x|R(x)|² = L|W|`. So orthogonality over all pairs of characters gives `Σ_{χ₁,χ₄∈W}|S|² ≤ L²Σ|G|² ≤ L⁴|W|² = q^{16/3}`.
  - Why it stops: for the sixth moment to save a power in `‖T′_W‖`, the off-diagonal sum `Σ_{χ₁≠χ₄}|S|²` must be at most `q^{5−δ}`. Orthogonality over all characters falls short by `q^{1/3} = L/|W|`, the density of `W`. If the restriction to `W × W` saved the density factor `|W|/L` in each variable, the bound would be `q^{14/3}`, B25's free value. What is needed is a restricted large sieve on `W × W`, for this `G`, that saves a little more than one of the two density factors. That is an uncertainty statement of B10's kind, for a function built from `R` itself.
  - Measured (`sixcycle_probe.py`). For random sets at `q = 499, 1009, 2003`, `tr((T_WT_W^*)³)` is within 4% of its free value `(5 − 6α + 2α²)L³|W|⁴`, `α = |W|/L` (gated at 6%). Divided by `L³|W|⁴`, the trace is, to within about 2% here, the off-diagonal share of `Σ|S|²` on `W × W` over the uniform share. Printed and not gated: the sets with the largest block sums exceed the free value by 14%, 26% and 32% at those three `q`, and two subgroup annihilators by 122% and 58%. The route can absorb an excess of almost `L/|W| = q^{1/3}`, but at these sizes `L/|W|` is only about `8` to `13`, so the numbers calibrate the conventions and do not separate the regimes.
- **B28. The gcd dichotomy for the critical block (round 559).** CLOSED as a route, recorded for its lemma [R24]. Group the pairs `(n, n′) ∈ I²` of `|D_I(χ)|²` by `g = gcd(n, n′)`, and write `n = ga`, `n′ = gb` with `(a, b) = 1`. Then `|D_I(χ)|² = Σ_g C_g(χ)`, where `C_g(χ) = Σ_{a,b∈I/g, (a,b)=1} χ(a)χ̄(b) = Σ_e μ(e)|D_{I/(ge)}(χ)|²` and `I/f = {m : fm ∈ I}`. So the part from the pairs with gcd above `G` is `P_{>G}(χ) = Σ_{f>G} c_f|D_{I/f}(χ)|²`, with `c_f = Σ_{g|f, g>G} μ(f/g)` and `|c_f| ≤ d(f)`. Every interval `I/f` in it has length below `N₀/G`.
  - The lemma. For any set `W`, Hölder (exponents `3/2` and `3`) and the mean value theorem for the cubes `D_{I/f}³` give `Σ_{χ∈W}|P_{>G}(χ)| ≤ q^{o(1)}|W|^{2/3}(q^{1/3}N₀ + N₀²/G)`. So at most `q^{1/2+o(1)} + q^{2/3−3d+o(1)}` characters take at least half of a large value from the pairs with gcd above `q^d`. At `|W| = q^{2/3}` that part carries a share at most `q^{o(1)}(q^{−1/18} + q^{−d})` of `|W|V₀²`. This sharpens B18's localization (heights between `q^{1/3−ε}` and `2q^{7/18}`): for every `d > 0` the reduced ratios of height below `2N₀q^{−d}` carry a share at most `q^{o(1)}(q^{−1/18} + q^{−d})`. It does not use Proposition 10.2's energy bound.
  - The pairs with gcd at most `G` have the fourth moment `Σ_χ|P_{≤G}(χ)|² = L·Σ_{(a,b)=1}#{g ≤ G : ga, gb ∈ I}²`, since `(2N₀)² < q`. That is about `qN₀² log G`, against `qN₀² log N₀` for `|D_I|⁴`. With `G` a power of `log q`, the critical count is `q^{2/3}·O(log log q)` in place of `q^{2/3}·O(log q)`.
  - Why it stops: a logarithm is not a power. The critical characters must take their large values from pairs with gcd at most `q^{o(1)}`. For gcd `1` that is the large spectrum of the Farey set `F* = {a/b : a, b ∈ I, (a, b) = 1}`, which has about `q^{7/9}` distinct elements of `F_q^*`, at level `|F*|^{5/7}`. Parseval gives `q^{2/3}` there, the same exponent, so this is core 1 again. Through additive characters, `Σ_{x∈F*}χ(x) = (τ(χ)/q)Σ_{t≠0}K(t)χ̄(t)` with `K(t) = Σ_{(a,b)=1}e(−tab̄/q)`, a bilinear sum of Kloosterman fractions. A bound on each `K(t)` is of Pólya–Vinogradov type and does not count large values.
  - Measured (`gcd_probe.py`, printed and not gated): for the 464 and 2154 characters with the largest `|D_I|` at `q = 10007` and `100003` (`N₀ = 36` and `88`), `Σ_W|P_{>1}|/Σ_W|D_I|²` is `0.69` and `0.77`. These sets are far from the critical regime, and there the lemma's bound exceeds the mass, since its logarithmic factors are not small at these sizes.
- **B29. The sixth moment by the dispersion method (round 560).** REDUCED to core 2, and closed in its bilinear form [R25]. At the critical block, SIX(δ) asks for the solutions of `x₁x₂x₃ − y₁y₂y₃ = ℓq` with `0 < |ℓ| ≤ 8q^{1/6}` and all variables in `I` to number `(N₀⁶/q)(1 + O(q^{−δ})) + O(q^{o(1)}N₀³)`. Write the equation as `Ax₃ − By₃ = ℓq` with `A = x₁x₂` and `B = y₁y₂`, weighted by `τ_I(A)τ_I(B)`. For `(A, B) = 1` there is at most one solution in `I²`, at `x₃ ≡ ℓq·Ā (mod B)`. Detecting it with additive characters mod `B` leaves, at each frequency `a = hℓq` with `|h| ≲ N₀`, the bilinear form `Bil(a) = Σ_{(A,B)=1} τ_I(A)τ_I(B)e(aĀ/B)`, with weight about `1/N₀`.
  - The main term is `q^{4/3}`. The trivial bound `|Bil(a)| ≤ N₀⁴` gives an error `q^{1/6}N₀⁴ = q^{31/18}`, larger by `N₀ = q^{7/18}`. So the route needs `|Bil(a)| ≤ N₀³q^{−δ}` on average over the frequencies.
  - Why it stops: up to logarithms, `N₀³ = ‖α‖‖β‖M^{1/2}` with `M = N₀²`. That is the sharp bound at one frequency for bilinear forms with Kloosterman fractions and arbitrary coefficients: one modulus `B` with `α_A = e(−aĀ/B)` attains it. So no single-frequency bound for arbitrary coefficients gives the power saving. The best balanced savings found are `M^{1/48}` (Duke–Friedlander–Iwaniec) and `M^{1/12}` (Dong–Robles–Zeindler, arXiv:2601.00292). Summed over the frequencies `a = hℓq`, the forms give back the original count exactly. So a trilinear bound would have to use the structure of those frequencies and of `τ_I`, which is SIX(δ) itself. This round did not test G11's trilinear bounds against it.
  - As an exponent of distribution: SIX(δ) is the mean-square distribution, with a power saving, of the balanced ternary divisor function `τ_{3,I}` in the residue classes mod `q` at length `x = N₀³`, so at `q = x^{6/7}`. For `d₃` with smooth weights, the Weyl bound of Petrow–Young and the fourth moment give `Σ_χ|L(½ + it, χ)|⁶ ≪ q^{4/3+ε}` and so such a statement for `q ≤ x^{3/4−ε}` (our computation; it is round 544's `θ = 1/3`, against the needed `θ < 1/6`). Aydemir–Boran (arXiv:2601.12601) state exponent `8/11` for `d₃` averaged over the residue classes mod a prime. The balanced box at exponent `θ` has side `q^{1/(3θ)}`: `q^{4/9}` at `3/4` and `q^{11/24}` at `8/11`, against `q^{7/18}` at `6/7`.

## C. The integral class and central values

- **C1. A fixed-modulus sixth moment.** CORE 3. Literature: no single-modulus bound better than `θ = 1/3` was found. Under GRH, Soundararajan's method gives `θ = 0`. Gao–Zhao (arXiv:2504.17905) reach `k ≤ 2` for a fixed modulus.
- **C2. Large central values at `q^{1/12}`.** CORE 4.
- **C3. Amplifying a set of characters.** CLOSED [R4]. With an amplifier of length `L` built from `R` characters, the off-diagonal is of random size `√(RL)` against the diagonal `L`. So it needs `L > R = q^{2/3}`, beyond the twist lengths of the twisted fourth moments surveyed (C7).
- **C4. One-sided distribution of `log|L|` (Radziwiłł–Soundararajan style).** CLOSED at this height. The deviation `log V = (log q)/12` is proportional to `log q`. With `P(χ) = Σ_{p≤x} χ(p)/√p`, moments of order `k < (log q)/(log x)` give savings of a power of `log q`, not of `q`.
- **C5. Fourth moments along cosets (Petrow–Young).** CLOSED for prime `q`: no coset of the characters mod `q` lowers the conductor.
- **C6. The half-plane together with subharmonicity of `log|L|`.** CLOSED. A zero at `β ≥ 5/7`, a large value at `½` and `|L| ≤ q^ε` on `Re s ≥ 11/12 + ε` are mutually compatible.
- **C7. Twisted fourth moments.** SURVEYED [R7, R13]. Core 2's Mellin form needs a twist of length `q^{7/18}`, with loss below `q^{1/6}`.
  - Blomer–Humphries–Khan–Milinovich (arXiv:1902.07042): "As an application, we obtain sharp upper bounds for the fourth moment twisted by the square of a Dirichlet polynomial of length q^{1/4}." In round 528's model, a bound of this shape at the detector's points gives the class-II line `1 − 4y(σ − ½)` for mollifiers up to `q^{1/8}`, without `FMx`'s `2x`. At `σ = 5/7` it still needs `y ≥ 7/18`, so the blocks contain `7/18`, a point of the barrier [R13a].
  - Zacharias (arXiv:1611.09582), prime `q`: error `(ℓ₁ℓ₂)^{3/2}L⁵q^{−η}` with `η = 1/14 − 3θ/7 = 11/448` at `θ = 7/64`.
  - Gao–Wu–Zhao (arXiv:2509.24690): an asymptotic for the fourth moment "mollified by a Dirichlet polynomial of length q^{1/22−ε}", for all moduli `q ≢ 2 (mod 4)`.
  - The `t`-aspect: Hughes–Young (arXiv:0709.2345), twists of length `T^{1/11−ε}`; Bettin–Bui–Li–Radziwiłł (arXiv:1609.02539), `T^{1/4−ε}`.
  - Gao–Zhao (arXiv:2507.18186): prime-power moduli.
- **C9. Core 4 through the approximate functional equation.** REDUCED to core 4 [R13]. It locates core 4's hard range.
  - A large `|L(½ + it, χ)| ≥ q^{1/12}` makes one dyadic piece `Σ_{n∼M} χ(n)n^{−1/2−it}`, `M ≤ q^{1/2}`, large.
  - Pieces with `M < q^{7/18}` are counted below `q^{2/3}` by the mean value of their cubes. The twisted bound of C7 reaches only `M ≤ q^{1/4}`.
  - At `M = q^{1/2}` the square has length `q` and value `q^{2/3}`, where the density term is exactly `2/3`. So powers of the piece cannot help there, and an unsquared count needs `LV(2/3, 2) < 4/3`. The mean value theorem gives `5/3`, Huxley `2` and Guth–Maynard's Theorem 1.1 `26/15`. For `ζ`, R12(d) records the database's exponent-pair bound above `2/3` in the count.
- **C10. `SIX(δ)` from equidistribution of `d₃` in every class.** CLOSED [R14b]. A saving `q^{−δ′}` in every class mod `q` gives `SIX(2δ′)`. At `X = N³ = q^{7/6}` that is level of distribution `6/7`. Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel (arXiv:1304.3199): "the exponent of distribution of the ternary divisor function d_3 in arithmetic progressions to prime moduli is at least 1/2+1/46"; and "when averaging over a fixed residue class", `1/2 + 1/34`.
- **C11. Upper bounds for product congruences in short intervals.** CLOSED [R15a]. Bourgain–Garaev–Konyagin–Shparlinski (arXiv:1203.0017, Theorem 19) bound the number `K_ν(p, h, s)` of solutions of `(x₁ + s)⋯(x_ν + s) ≡ (y₁ + s)⋯(y_ν + s) ≢ 0 (mod p)`, `1 ≤ x_i, y_i ≤ h`, by `(h^ν/p^{ν/e_ν} + 1)h^ν exp(c(ν) log h/log log h)`, with `e_ν = max{ν² − 2ν − 2, ν² − 3ν + 4}`. For `ν = 3`, `e₃ = 4`, and at `h = q^{7/18}` the bound is `q^{19/12+o(1)}`, above the expected `q^{4/3}`. `SIX(δ)` needs more than an upper bound. `χ₀` removes `N⁶/(q − 1)`, so a bound `C·N⁶/q` with `C > 1` leaves `(C − 1)N⁶`. What is needed is an asymptotic with a power-saving error.
- **C12. Moments averaged over the moduli.** CLOSED [R15b]. Chandee–Li–Matomäki–Radziwiłł (arXiv:2409.01457) recall that "In 1970, Huxley obtained a sharp upper bound for the sixth moment of Dirichlet L-functions at the central point, averaged over primitive characters χ modulo q and all moduli q ≤ Q". For one modulus, an average bound `Q^{2+o(1)}` gives at most `q^{2+o(1)}`: loss `1`, against the Weyl bound's `1/3` and core 3's `1/6`.
- **C13. Halász's mean-value theorem (pretentious distance) for the critical sums.** CLOSED. Halász bounds `Σ_{n≤N} f(n)` by `N(1 + M)e^{−M}` plus a small term, where `M` is the pretentious distance `D(f, n^{it}; N)²` minimized over `t`. Since `D² ≤ Σ_{p≤N} 2/p = 2 log log N + O(1)`, the bound saves at most a power of `log N`. `|Σ_{n∼N} χ(n)| ≥ N^{5/7}` needs a saving `N^{2/7}`.
- **C8. Variance of `d₃` in progressions to a single modulus.** SURVEYED [R8]. Nguyen (arXiv:2205.02354, Corollary 1) treats `X = d^c` with `c ∈ (k − 1, k)`. For `k = 3` that is `X ∈ (d², d³)`. It records Kowalski–Ricotta's prime-modulus range as `c ∈ (k − ½, k)`. `SIX(δ)` sits at `X = q^{7/6}`.
- **C14. A twisted second moment with a long twist (round 552).** REDUCED to core 2 [R17b]. Let `S_χ(N)` be the smooth sum of length `N = q^{7/18}`. If `|S_χ(N)| ≥ N^{5/7}`, then `|L(½ + it, χ)| ≥ q^{1/12−ε}` for some `|t| ≤ q^ε`. So H\*'s simplest instance follows from `Σ_χ |L(½ + it, χ)|²|S_χ(N)|⁴ ≪ q^{1+η+ε}N²`, averaged over `|t| ≤ q^ε`, with `η < 1/6`: the count is then at most `q^{1/2+η+ε}`. The twist `S_χ(N)²` has length `q^{7/9}`.
  - The large sieve gives `η = 5/18`. The route needs a saving of `q^{1/9}` over it.
  - Bui–Pratt–Robles–Zaharescu (arXiv:1808.10803) study the second moment "twisted by the square of an arbitrary Dirichlet polynomial", and "obtain an asymptotic formula provided that the length of the Dirichlet polynomial is less than q^{51/101} = q^{1/2 +1/202}".
  - Through Mellin, the same bound gives `Σ_χ |S_χ(N)|⁶ ≪ q^{1+η+ε}N³`, which is `SIX(1/6 − η)`. So the route is at least as strong as core 2.

## D. Restructuring the argument

- **D1. Another Linnik deduction.** CLOSED within the density framework. Zeros at `σ` contribute `q^{(A(σ)−L)(1−σ)}`, so the deduction needs `L > A(5/7)`.
- **D2. An auxiliary composite modulus `qr` (`r` prime to `q`), to borrow Chen–Gupta–Li's subdivision.** CLOSED [R2].
  - The characters mod `q` form the subgroup `X(q)` of `X(qr)`, and each coset of `X(r)` holds one of them. For `χ ≠ ψ` mod `q`, `χψ̄` has conductor `q` in any lift. So no subdivision of `X(qr)` into cosets lowers the conductor of the off-diagonal.
  - For the subdivided case, SEVEN-THIRDS §2 quotes "|W| ⪅ (qT)^ϵ q q₁^{1/3} T N^{2−4σ}". With modulus `qr = q^{1+η}` and `q₁ = q` that term is `q^{4/3+η}N^{2−4σ}`, or `q^{2/3+η}` at the critical point. `W` lies in one of the `r` cosets of `X(q)`, and there the term is `q^{4/3}N^{2−4σ}`, as it is mod `q` directly.
- **D3. A larger `T` for subdivision in `t`.** CLOSED [R2]. A `t`-interval of length `T₁ ≥ 1` has conductor `qT₁ ≥ q`. In the same term the factor `qT` grows with `T`, and so the term never drops below its value at `T = q^{o(1)}`.
- **D4. Cancellation over the characters in Linnik's deduction (round 557).** CLOSED as a route [R22c]. The deduction bounds `Σ_{χ≠χ₀}χ̄(a)Σ_ρ x^ρŵ(ρ)` by absolute values. With `q^{2/3}` critical characters having zeros at `β = 5/7`, the absolute bound is `q^{2/3}x^{5/7}`, which is `x` at `x = q^{7/3}`. Keeping the phases `χ̄(a)`, an exponent `7/3 − δ` needs a saving `q^{2δ/7}` in the twisted sum, for every residue `a`.
  - Parseval over `a` gives that saving for all but about `q^{1/3+4δ/7}` residues. Linnik's problem needs every one.
  - Averaging over `x` damps the zeros with `|γ| ≫ 1/(η log x)`, but not those near the real axis.
  - For the worst `a` the near-real zeros give a weighted `R(a)` over the critical set, and the energy bound gives only `max_{a≠1}|R(a)| ≤ (κ − 1)^{1/4}|W|`, no saving when `κ ≤ q^{o(1)}`.

## E. Structure particular to prime moduli

- **E1. Additive combinatorics in `X(q) ≅ ℤ/(q − 1)` (Freiman-type inverse theorems).** CLOSED at the critical point. Proposition 10.2 gives `W` minimal energy, so there is no additive structure to exploit. Darbar–Kerr–Munsch–Shparlinski (arXiv:2604.02960) prove "new zero-density estimates on average over sets of characters with a small product set". A set with a small product set has large energy, the opposite of the critical `W`.
- **E2. The arithmetic of Gauss and Jacobi sums (Stickelberger, Gross–Koblitz, Katz's equidistribution).** REDUCED to B12. These results concern sums over all characters, or over algebraic families. `W` is an arbitrary set of `q^{2/3}` characters, so they reach `W` only through a structure theorem for the sets on which `S₃` is large, which is B12. For scale, at the critical point [R17c]: discarding the Jacobi phases gives `N^{3/2}q² = q^{19/6}` (`seven_thirds.py` check (P)), and Chen–Gupta–Li's bound is `q³`. If the normalized phases `J(ψ₁, ψ₂)/√q` on the `q²` pairs behaved like independent random signs, `S₃` would be about `N^{3/2}q = q^{13/6}`. That is a heuristic, not a bound. *(Round 553: the survey behind this entry missed results over arbitrary sets. Lu–Zheng (arXiv:2005.14358) prove equidistribution of normalized Jacobi sums when the characters "run through arbitrary sets of nontrivial multiplicative characters", under `#A₁ ≥ q^{1/2+ε}` and `#A₂ ≥ (log q)^{1/δ−1}`, or `#A₁#A₂/q → ∞`. Lu–Zheng–Zheng (arXiv:1305.3405) and Xi (arXiv:1809.04286) prove earlier forms. These reach `W` directly. For fixed `χ₂` and `m`, the Jacobi sums in `S₃` form a weighted bilinear sum over the product set `(Wχ̄₂) × (χ₂W̄)`, and Parseval already saves `q^{1/6}` there. Summed over `χ₂ ∈ W` and the dual variable `m`, that bound gives `Nq|W|² = q^{28/9} > q³` [R18]. The shortfall is in the outer sums, which is core 1, so the reduction to B12 stands.)*
- **E3. Large smooth sums from aligned values `χ(p)` on small primes.** REDUCED to core 4: through Mellin, a large smooth sum is a large `L(½ + it, χ)`.
- **E4. Poisson summation on the critical smooth sums.** CLOSED: the dual has length `q^{11/18}`, at height `q^{7/18}`, and the fourth moment then gives `q^{8/9}`. *(Round 552: that is the mean value theorem's bound for the dual's fourth power, whose term `M⁴` is the principal character's. Poisson summation is an isometry here, `|S_χ(N)| = (N/√q)|D(χ̄)|` with `D` the dual sum, so the dual's exact fourth moment gives back `q^{2/3}` [R17d]. Either way there is no gain.)*
- **E5. Subgroups of `X(q)` (the order structure of `q − 1`).** CLOSED: Gram entries on a coset `χ · H_d` are `M(η)` with `η` still of conductor `q`.
- **E6. The `q`-analogue of van der Corput and the Postnikov–Korobov method (round 552).** CLOSED for prime `q`: these methods factor the modulus. Irving (arXiv:1503.07156): "We use the q-analogue of van der Corput's method to estimate short character sums to smooth moduli". Banks–Shparlinski (arXiv:1605.07553) derive "new bounds on short character sums when the modulus q has a small core", the core being the product of the primes dividing `q`. Chang (arXiv:1201.0299): "We establish new estimates on short character sums for arbitrary composite moduli with small prime factors". A prime `q` is its own core and is not `q^δ`-smooth.
- **E7. Base change to an imaginary quadratic field `K` (round 552).** CLOSED. The Hecke characters of `K` have an angular aspect, where subdivision is available. But for `q` unramified in `K`, the base change `χ ∘ N_{K/ℚ}` of `χ mod q` has conductor of norm `q²`, and `L(s, χ ∘ N) = L(s, χ)L(s, χχ_K)`. For `χ ≠ χ′` the Gram entry of the base-change family is a smooth sum of `(χχ̄′)(n)r_K(n)`, `r_K = 1 ∗ χ_K`, with dual length about `q²/N`, against `q/N` mod `q`. The base changes all have angular type `0`, so subdividing in that aspect does not split them.
- **E8. The polynomial method for additive shifts (round 556).** CLOSED for sets without multiplicative structure. A line sum of SEVEN-THIRDS §6 is `Σ_{χ₂∈W}Σ_x A_{χ₂}(x)B_{χ₂}(αx + β)`, with `α = −m₁/m₂` and `β = −m₃/m₂`. Here `A_{χ₂}(x) = χ̄₂(x)R(x)` and `B_{χ₂}(y) = χ₂(y)R̄(y)` have multiplicative spectra in `χ̄₂W` and `χ₂W̄`, so each term is an additive correlation of two functions with flat multiplicative spectra.
  - Stepanov's polynomial method is the standard tool for intersections of a multiplicative subgroup with its additive shifts. It rests on the subgroup of order `d` being the zero set of `x^d − 1`.
  - A set `W` of minimal energy has no description of that kind, and `A_{χ₂}`, `B_{χ₂}` are not indicators of subgroups. So the method does not reach the line sums.
